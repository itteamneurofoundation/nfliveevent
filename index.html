The Content: The Rashomon Effect in Healthcare

"Rashomon", the film directed by Akira Kurosawa and released in 1950, introduced the world to the "Rashomon effect"—a term used when different eyewitnesses give contradictory accounts of the same event.

A single clinical encounter, a diagnosis delivered, a complication explained is remembered differently by the doctor who gave it, the patient who received it, and the family who stood beside them. None of these versions is a lie. Each is shaped by fear, hope, medical training, or grief.

This session explores practical approaches to communication and consent that acknowledge this gap before it widens into mistrust. Come prepared to recognise a story you have lived through more than once—from more than one side.

The Purpose..

Medicine trains us with rare precision in diagnosis, in techniques and in the discipline of healing. Yet beyond the clinic lies a world our training rarely touches: the psychology of trust, the building of institutions, the ethics of growth, communication, Finance, and now, the quiet arrival of AI reshaping medicine itself.

This program is designed to bridge that gap. Each month, over lunch on the third Sunday, we turn to one such dimension—not as a lecture, but as a conversation among colleagues, each carrying experience the other has yet to discover.

This is an invitation to look, together, at the parts of our profession we have long navigated alone.
